Choosing a conveyancer or solicitor to sell your home

Your agent markets the home and negotiates with buyers. Your conveyancer or solicitor handles the legal side, from the contract to settlement. Because a NSW home can’t be marketed until the contract is ready, choosing one is usually among the first things to do. This guide explains what they do, how conveyancers and solicitors differ, and how to choose. It’s general information for Newcastle and Lake Macquarie sellers, not legal advice.

What they do when you sell

  • Prepare the contract for sale and attach the required documents, such as the title search, plan, drainage diagram and council planning certificate. See Getting your NSW contract for sale ready before you list.
  • Advise on special conditions, such as the settlement period, early access or a sale subject to you buying elsewhere.
  • Handle exchange, including the cooling-off period and the deposit, which is held in a trust account. See cooling-off & exchange.
  • Answer the buyer’s questions (requisitions) and deal with their solicitor or conveyancer.
  • Work with your lender to discharge your mortgage at settlement. See mortgage discharge for sellers.
  • Calculate adjustments for council rates, water and any strata levies.
  • Complete settlement electronically. NSW settlements happen on the eConveyancing platform, and both parties need a solicitor or conveyancer who uses it. See settlement day checklist.

Conveyancer or solicitor?

The NSW Government says conveyancers and solicitors are equally qualified to do conveyancing. The difference is in their wider scope:

  • Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transfers and must be licensed by NSW Fair Trading.
  • Solicitors can also advise on wider legal issues around the sale.

A straightforward sale is commonly handled by either. If your sale involves a deceased estate, a separation or property settlement, a dispute, a boundary problem, a subdivision or complex tax questions, you may want a solicitor who can advise on those issues too. See selling a deceased estate and selling after separation or divorce.

Both conveyancers and solicitors must hold professional indemnity insurance, which protects you if they make a mistake.

How to check who you’re hiring

  • Conveyancers: check their licence with the NSW Government’s licence check (Verify NSW). It shows the licence status, the category of licence and any disciplinary action.
  • Solicitors: the Law Society of NSW’s Find a Lawyer service lists solicitors and shows whether they can practise.
  • Use the name, firm and licence or practising details they give you, and check them yourself.

Getting a quote

  • Ask for a written quote that separates the professional fee from disbursements (the cost of searches, certificates and other third-party charges).
  • Ask what’s not included, for example extra work if the sale falls over, the settlement is delayed or the contract needs to be redone.
  • Ask when fees are payable and whether anything is charged if the property doesn’t sell.
  • Compare what’s included, not just the headline price. See selling costs and timeline in the Hunter.

Questions to ask before you engage one

  • Who will handle my file day to day, and how will you keep me updated?
  • How soon can you have my contract ready for the agent?
  • Have you handled sales like mine recently: strata, acreage, a deceased estate, a tenanted property?
  • Are you familiar with City of Newcastle or Lake Macquarie City Council certificates, Hunter Water diagrams and Hunter issues such as mine subsidence and flood planning?
  • What do you need from me now, and what could slow things down?
  • What happens if the buyer asks for a later settlement, or settlement is delayed?

Working alongside your agent

Your agent and your conveyancer or solicitor work best as a team. The agent needs to know when the contract will be ready, and will pass on buyer questions about terms. But agents can’t give legal advice. Anything that changes the contract, such as special conditions, a shorter cooling-off period or a changed settlement date, should be confirmed with your solicitor or conveyancer before you agree to it.

If something goes wrong

Raise concerns with the firm first. Complaints about licensed conveyancers can be made to NSW Fair Trading. Complaints about solicitors go to the Office of the Legal Services Commissioner (NSW).

FAQ

Do I need a conveyancer or solicitor to sell a house in NSW?

In practice, yes. The contract for sale has to be prepared before the home is marketed, and NSW settlements are done electronically, with each party represented by a solicitor or conveyancer who uses the eConveyancing platform.

What is the difference between a conveyancer and a solicitor?

The NSW Government says both are equally qualified to do conveyancing. Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transfers. Solicitors can also advise on wider legal issues, such as estates, family law or disputes.

How do I check a conveyancer is licensed in NSW?

Use the NSW Government’s licence check (Verify NSW). It shows the licence status, category and any disciplinary action. For solicitors, use the Law Society of NSW’s Find a Lawyer service.

When should I engage a conveyancer when selling?

Before you list. In NSW a home can’t be offered for sale until the contract has been prepared, so engaging early helps the campaign start on time.

Can my real estate agent give me legal advice about the contract?

No. Your agent can pass on buyer requests and explain the process, but anything that changes the contract should be confirmed with your solicitor or conveyancer.
