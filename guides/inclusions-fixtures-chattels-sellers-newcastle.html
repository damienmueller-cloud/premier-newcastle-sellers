Inclusions, fixtures & chattels for sellers (Newcastle & the Hunter)

When you sell, buyers want a clear answer to a simple question: what stays with the house? This plain-English guide covers fixtures vs chattels and inclusions at a high level, why sellers should list inclusions early, common inclusion themes (qualitative only), exclusions that often need explicit contract wording, open-home and marketing honesty, appraisal questions, and five-council qualitative framing — across Newcastle and the Hunter. It is not legal advice. It invents no fee amounts, day-count guarantees, dollar figures, commission percentages, medians, ROI, clearance rates, days-on-market averages, brand “must include” claims or “#1” rankings. Your solicitor or conveyancer leads contract wording on inclusions and exclusions; your agent helps with campaign honesty and buyer communication.

Fixtures, chattels and inclusions — high-level framing

Education themes only — confirm labels and contract wording with your solicitor:

  • Fixtures — items commonly treated as part of the property because they are fixed to the land or building (exact tests are solicitor-led).
  • Chattels — movable items that may or may not convey with the sale; do not assume they stay unless agreed in writing.
  • Inclusions — the practical marketing and contract list of what the buyer is getting. Clarity here reduces later disputes.

Related framing: Preparing your home to sell · Cooling-off & exchange for sellers.

Why list inclusions early

  • Avoid last-minute arguments about dishwashers, curtains, garden pieces, pool gear or solar after an offer lands.
  • Keep the campaign honest — buyers and their solicitors compare photos, open-home experience and the written inclusions list.
  • Give your solicitor time to draft clear special conditions or exclusions before exchange.

Common inclusion themes (qualitative — not “must include” rules)

These are conversation starters only. None are automatic rules; confirm in writing with your solicitor:

  • Fixed appliances — cooktops, ovens, dishwashers and similar fixed kitchen or laundry items often sit in inclusion talks.
  • Built-ins — wardrobes, shelving and other built-in joinery are commonly discussed as part of the property story.
  • Curtains / blinds / window treatments — often negotiated; say early if you intend to take them.
  • Garden fixtures — fixed outdoor structures and fittings can blur with movable pots or décor; clarify before photos.
  • Pool equipment — pumps, cleaners and related gear may be treated differently to freestanding items; pair with pool compliance certificate themes where a pool or spa exists.
  • Solar — panels, inverters and related systems often need explicit inclusion/exclusion wording — solicitor-led. Deeper owned vs leased/PPA and battery themes: Solar panels & home batteries when selling.
  • EV charger — wall-mounted chargers are increasingly discussed; confirm ownership and whether they convey.

Hard limit: this site invents no brand claims, must-include lists, fee tables or dollar amounts. Settlement and any deposit run through lawyers/conveyancers and trust accounts — never as Stripe, card checkout or online property payments on this site.

Exclusions and specials that often need explicit wording

  • Anything you plan to remove, replace or gift separately before settlement.
  • Items that look fixed in photos but you consider personal (art, some lighting, garden décor).
  • Shared or unusual setups (strata storage, rented appliances, leased solar) — solicitor and agent eyes early.

Verbal “we’ll sort it later” deals are a common dispute theme. Put the list in the contract pack your solicitor prepares. Related: Multiple offers & negotiating for sellers.

Open homes and marketing honesty

  • Photos and video should match what you intend to convey — if an item will not stay, do not feature it as if it will.
  • Open-home answers should align with the written inclusions list your solicitor and agent are using.
  • If something changes mid-campaign, update marketing and the contract list promptly.

FAQ

What is the difference between fixtures, chattels and inclusions when I sell?

At a high level, fixtures are often treated as part of the property (things fixed to the land or building), while chattels are movable items that may or may not convey. “Inclusions” is the practical listing and contract language for what the buyer is getting. Exact labels and what must be written into your contract are solicitor-led. Education only — not legal advice.

Why should sellers list inclusions early?

Listing inclusions early reduces disputes late in a campaign, keeps marketing honest (photos matching what conveys), and gives your solicitor time to draft clear special conditions or exclusions. Surprises about dishwashers, curtains, pool gear or solar after an offer can stall negotiations. Education only.

What kinds of items are commonly discussed as inclusions?

Qualitative themes often include fixed appliances, built-ins, curtains and blinds, garden fixtures, pool equipment, solar systems and EV chargers — none of these are automatic “must include” rules. What stays vs what you keep is property-specific and should be confirmed in writing with your solicitor. This site invents no brand claims or must-include lists. Education only.

What about exclusions and special conditions?

Items you intend to remove, replace or negotiate separately often need explicit contract wording — solicitor-led. Do not rely on verbal side agreements alone. Your agent helps with campaign honesty; your solicitor leads the contract. Education only — not legal advice.

Do inclusions rules differ across Newcastle, Lake Macquarie, Maitland, Cessnock and Port Stephens?

Contract themes around fixtures, chattels and inclusions are broadly consistent across NSW residential sales, but local practice and how agents/solicitors document lists can still vary. Confirm with your solicitor for your LGA. Invent no medians, clearance rates or days-on-market averages. Education only.

What should I ask at an appraisal about inclusions?

Ask how the agent usually documents inclusions in marketing and the contract pack, how photos should match what conveys, how exclusions are flagged early, and when your solicitor should lock the list. Legal wording stays with your solicitor. Education only.
