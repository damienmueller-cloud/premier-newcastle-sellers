NSW planning certificates (section 10.7 / s149) when selling in Newcastle & the Hunter

When you sell in NSW, a planning certificate — often called a section 10.7 certificate; some sellers still say s149 from older talk — commonly sits in the contract or disclosure pack. This plain-English guide helps Newcastle & Hunter sellers understand what that document is for at a high level — awareness, not scare. It is not legal advice, not planning advice, not financial advice and not insurance advice. Your solicitor or conveyancer leads reading and what must accompany the sale. It complements our flood & bushfire, contaminated land / UST, mine / subsidence, easements, restrictive covenants / 88B, heritage / character, unapproved works and vacant land guides without duplicating them — those pages cover theme detail; this page covers the certificate pack itself.

What a planning certificate means in seller plain language

In everyday seller talk, a planning certificate is a council-issued document that buyers, conveyancers and lenders often expect to see with the sale file. Common names you may hear:

  • Planning certificate — the plain description used throughout this guide
  • Section 10.7 certificate — the name many NSW sellers and agents use today
  • s149 — older seller talk some people still use; same high-level idea of a council planning certificate in the pack

Exact certificate types, optional vs standard packs, what notations mean and what must sit beside a contract are solicitor-led. This site invents no statute hard-quotes, Act body text, ordinance numbers, LEP/DCP clause numbers, flood maps, BAL scores, EPA lists, contamination maps, risk scores, insurance premiums, fee tables, day-count guarantees or commission percentages.

Solicitor leads reading (agent helps with honesty)

Having a planning certificate in the pack is different from DIY-interpreting every line. Calm process usually looks like:

  • Gather what you already have — current or prior planning certificates, solicitor notes, title searches and any related specialist reports that sit with the sale file.
  • Do not invent answers — if you are unsure what a notation means, defer to your solicitor (and specialists when a theme guide says they lead).
  • Agents help with campaign honesty — share known documents early so photos, copy and open-home answers stay aligned; agents do not interpret certificates as advice from this page.
  • Legal pack first — what must sit in contract documents is solicitor-led — not social-media-led.

This page invents no statute hard-quotes, guarantee timelines, fee tables or “guaranteed clean certificate” claims.

Auction vs private treaty when certificate diligence matters

Both methods use the same honesty and disclosure posture. Auction can concentrate competition when qualified buyers are ready and documents are clear; private treaty can suit longer diligence when buyers want more time with solicitors or lenders reviewing the certificate pack. Unclear certificate themes can soften some buyer confidence until the pack is clearer — ask your agent which method fits your pocket, then confirm contract wording with your solicitor.
