How to choose a real estate agent in Newcastle

Choosing who lists your home is one of the highest-leverage seller decisions. This guide is an honest interview checklist for Newcastle and the wider Hunter — no fabricated rankings, no pressure to sign on the spot.

Start with who will actually run your campaign

Ask early: who appraises, who writes the campaign plan, who runs opens, who follows up buyers, and who calls you with updates. A polished brand name means little if your file is handed to a junior you never meet. At Premier Estate Agents (75a Regent Street, New Lambton), seller enquiries from this site go to directors Rodney Goodwin and Vlado Zvicer, with apartment specialist Stephen Cornes available for units — not a call-centre queue that auctions your lead.

Interview questions every seller should ask

  • What recent comparable sales inform your price range for my street and property type — and can you walk me through them?
  • Who will be on-site at opens and who owns buyer follow-up after each open?
  • How often will we speak during the campaign, and by what channel (call, text, email)?
  • Would you recommend auction or private treaty for this property, and why?
  • What is included in marketing — photography, floor plan, online portals, signboard, print — and what is optional?
  • How do you present fees and commission in writing before I sign an agency agreement?
  • What would make you advise waiting to list (presentation, seasonality, competing stock)?

Local knowledge vs national brand

National or franchise brands can bring systems and brand recognition. Local independents can bring street-level buyer memory and faster decision-making. Neither is automatically better. What matters is evidence: recent sales in your pocket of Newcastle (or Lake Macquarie, Maitland, Cessnock, Port Stephens), a clear plan for your buyer pool, and someone accountable by name. Ask for suburb-specific examples, not state-wide slogans.

  • Inner Newcastle / harbour vs beachside vs family suburbs trade differently — ask which pocket they know best.
  • Across the five Hunter councils, lifestyle, commute and buyer pools shift; generic “Hunter market” talk is a weak answer.
  • Prefer agents who say what they do not specialise in as clearly as what they do.

Auction vs private treaty experience

Method should follow property and buyer pool, not habit. Ask how many recent campaigns they ran as auction vs private treaty in your property type, what clearance or negotiation outcomes looked like, and how they would switch method mid-campaign if enquiry is soft. See also Auction vs private treaty in the Hunter.

Marketing plan clarity

A useful marketing plan names channels, creative, timing, open-home cadence and how feedback rolls into price strategy. Vague promises of “maximum exposure” are not a plan. Ask to see a sample campaign calendar and who pays for each line item before you sign.

Fees and commission — transparency without invented numbers

Agency fees and marketing costs vary by property, method and negotiated agreement. We do not publish fabricated commission percentages on this site. What you should insist on in writing: the commission rate or structure, what marketing costs are fixed vs optional, when fees become payable, and how GST is shown. Compare apples-to-apples across agents — a lower headline rate with expensive mandatory marketing can cost more overall. Related: Selling costs and timeline in the Hunter.

Communication cadence

Agree upfront how often you will hear from the agent: after every open, weekly summary, or same-day for serious offers. Silence after opens is a common seller frustration. Ask who covers holidays and who you call if something feels off mid-campaign.

Open-home strategy

Ask how many opens they propose in week one, how they qualify buyers, how they handle private inspections, and how they protect your home (shoes, valuables, neighbour notice). Strong open strategy pairs with disciplined buyer follow-up — not just a busy Saturday roster.

Comparable sales process

Price guidance should rest on recent, tightly matched sales — similar dwelling type, land/size, condition and location — not a suburb median that mixes houses with units. Ask the agent to explain adjustments (renovations, aspect, parking, flood/strata issues) rather than hand you a single “worth” number. See Understanding comparable sales in the Hunter.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed sale prices with no market context, or pressure to sign the same day.
  • “We’re #1” claims with no verifiable criteria (or criteria you cannot check).
  • Vague answers about who works your file day-to-day.
  • Refusal to put fees, marketing and method in clear writing.
  • Overpricing to win the listing, then pushing for large cuts within days.
  • Talking only about their brand, never about your street’s recent buyers.
