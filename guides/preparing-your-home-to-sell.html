Preparing your home to sell

Preparation does not mean spending a fortune. It means removing friction for buyers and presenting a property that photographs and shows well.

If the property has a swimming pool or spa, raise pool compliance certificate themes early with your solicitor and a licensed certifier (education only; not legal advice). List inclusions, fixtures & chattels early so photos and the contract match what conveys (education only; not legal advice). If the home has rooftop solar or a battery, see solar panels & home batteries when selling (education only; not energy advice). Mine or ground-condition themes? See mine / coal-mining subsidence awareness (education only; not engineering advice). Unapproved works or building-certificate themes? See unapproved renovations & building certificates (education only; not legal advice). Selling vacant land uses different prep themes — access, services and site presentation rather than house inclusions (education only; not legal advice). Selling a townhouse often adds parking, courtyard and strata prep themes (education only; not legal advice).

Clarify your timeline first

ASAP, 1–3 months, or researching only? Timeline shapes whether you do light tidy-up, targeted repairs, or hold for a seasonal window. Share this when you request an appraisal.

Presentation basics that usually help

Declutter surfaces and storage, complete small unfinished repairs, deep-clean kitchens and bathrooms, maximise natural light, and keep gardens tidy. Ask your agent what matters most for your property type.

Documents & logistics

Gather rates notices, strata info (if applicable), and any building reports you already have. Confirm access for photography and opens. Good logistics shorten the path from appraisal to campaign.
